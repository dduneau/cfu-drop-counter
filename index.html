Method


This tab describes how the Load step estimates the bacterial load of each fly. It gives the R code that defines the estimator, and an R example that reproduces the app's per-fly table.


Model


Each fly's homogenate is plated as drops at several dilutions. For drop i of a fly, let vᵢ be the volume plated (µL) and dᵢ the dilution as a fraction (1 = undiluted, 1/25 = diluted 25-fold).


The number of colonies yᵢ in the drop is modelled as Poisson, with mean


μᵢ = λ · vᵢ · dᵢ


Here λ is the load per µL of homogenate, the one unknown for that fly. Drops are assumed independent.


Which drops enter the estimate


    

  • Drops marked Ignore drop (NA in the counts file) are left out.
    • 

  • Drops in a row or column marked as not plated (dilution NA) are left out.
    • 

  • Drops marked Too many (Inf) are treated as above the ceiling.
    • 

  • Dilutions are entered as factors (10 = 1/10). A factor of 1 or less is treated as undiluted, and the app warns about factors between 0 and 1.
    • 



Drops above the ceiling


Colonies merge in crowded drops, so counts above a ceiling M (30 by default) are not read as exact. The setting Drops with more colonies than can be read decides what such a drop contributes to the likelihood:

Each probability is that of a Poisson variable Y with mean μᵢ. In R's function the first two settings are ignoreAboveMaxCFU = FALSE and ignoreAboveMaxCFU = TRUE, with maxCFU = M; the third is maxCFU = NA.


Estimate and standard error


    

  • The estimate. λ̂ maximises the log-likelihood over all the fly's drops. It is found as the root of the score with respect to log λ, using R's uniroot with the interval extended as needed.
    • 

  • The standard error of log λ̂. It is 1 / √(−ℓ″), where ℓ″ is the second derivative of the log-likelihood with respect to log λ, taken at the estimate (the observed information). It is reported on the log scale chosen (log10 or log2).
    • 

  • The load per fly is λ̂ × V, where V is the homogenate volume per fly (250 µL by default).
    • 

  • The detection limit per fly is V / Σ vᵢ dᵢ, summed over all of that fly's drops. It is the load implied by a single colony over everything plated for that fly. It is not the load at which detection becomes reliable.
    • 



Status of each fly


    

  • Detected: a load was estimated.
    • 

  • Not detected: no colony on any drop of the fly, so no load could be estimated.
    • 

  • Above the dilutions used: no drop was countable, so the load is higher than the series can measure.
    • 

  • Estimation failed: colonies were counted, but the estimator found no estimate.
    • 



Assumptions to keep in mind


    

  • The standard error assumes pure Poisson variation between drops. Extra variation, from pipetting or clumped bacteria, makes the real uncertainty larger than this standard error.
    • 

  • Counts at or below the ceiling are taken as exact. If crowded drops near the ceiling are undercounted, the estimate inherits that error. A lower ceiling limits it.
    • 



Why drops above the ceiling are kept by default


The two ways of handling the ceiling were compared on simulated flies of known load, using the lab's design:


    

  • One fly per row, 10 drops of 5 µL at dilutions 1, 1/10, 1/100, 1/5, 1/25, 1/125, 1/3, 1/9, 1/27 and 1/81.
    • 

  • A ceiling of 30 and a homogenate of 250 µL.
    • 

  • Counts drawn from the Poisson model. Drops above 300 colonies were recorded as too many to count.
    • 

  • 2,000 flies at each true load, each estimated both ways.
    •

Over this range both settings are unbiased to within about 0.02 log10. Keeping the drops above the ceiling gives errors as small or smaller, because every drop contributes.


Near the top of the measurable range, the two settings differ sharply.


    

  • At 10⁵·² CFU per fly, about 4 flies in 10 have a single countable drop.
    • 

  • In a run of 4,000 flies at that load, leaving the drops above the ceiling out gave an estimate more than 10 times too high for 2% of the flies estimated (86 of 3,746), and more than twice too high for 6% (216). Keeping them never did.
    • 

  • The reason: when the drops above the ceiling are left out, the one remaining count is treated as a count known to be at most 30. A count of, say, 25 under that condition fits a very high load almost as well as the true one. When the drops are kept, the 25 is an ordinary count, and the drops above the ceiling add that the load is high. Together they pin the load down.
    • 



Above about 10⁵·⁴ CFU per fly, most flies have no countable drop at all. Both settings then report them as Above the dilutions used.


What keeping them costs. It takes the counts just below the ceiling at face value. When crowded drops were made to undercount by about 15% at 30 colonies, keeping them gave a bias of −0.07 log10 at 10⁴ CFU per fly, against −0.05 for leaving them out. A lower ceiling reduces this.


Both simulations, the comparison over loads and the run near the top of the range, are part of the app's source code (tests/ceiling_simulation.js), so they can be rerun or adapted to another design.


The estimator in R


The app runs a JavaScript translation of the two R functions below, line by line, including R's root finder and its Poisson probability functions. The translation was checked against this R code, run in R 4.6:


    

  • On 1,415 simulated samples under every setting, the two agree to about 10⁻¹² and succeed or fail on the same samples.
    • 

  • On a 48-fly, 8-plate data set, counts, statuses and detection limits are identical, and the loads agree to 10⁻¹³.
    • 



loadEstimate.R
loadEstimateOneSample <- function (cfu, dilution, volume, maxCFU, ignoreAboveMaxCFU, logbase = NA)
{
    l <- !is.na(cfu)
    dilution <- dilution[l]
    cfu <- cfu[l]
    vdilution <- dilution * volume
    if (length(cfu) <= 0) {
        warning("No CFU counts to work with!")
        return(list(logload = NA, se.logload = NA, load = NA,
            se.load = NA, code = -1, sumCFU = 0, maxCFU = maxCFU,
            ignoreAboveMaxCFU = ignoreAboveMaxCFU))
    }
    if (length(cfu) != length(dilution)) {
        stop("cfu and dilution should have the same length")
    }
    if (any(cfu < 0)) {
        stop("CFU counts must be positive")
    }
    if (any(dilution <= 0)) {
        stop("Dilution factors must be strinctly positive")
    }
    if (any(dilution > 1)) {
        stop("Dilution cannot exceed one")
    }
    if (!is.na(maxCFU)) {
        belowMaxCFU <- cfu <= maxCFU
        cfu.below <- cfu[belowMaxCFU]
        vdilution.below <- vdilution[belowMaxCFU]
        n.below <- length(cfu.below)
    }
    else {
        belowMaxCFU <- is.finite(cfu)
        cfu.below <- cfu[belowMaxCFU]
        vdilution.below <- vdilution[belowMaxCFU]
        n.below <- length(cfu.below)
    }
    if (!is.na(maxCFU)) {
        aboveMaxCFU <- cfu > maxCFU
        vdilution.above <- vdilution[aboveMaxCFU]
        n.above <- length(vdilution.above)
    }
    else {
        aboveMaxCFU <- NULL
        vdilution.above <- NULL
        n.above <- 0
    }
    sum_cfu <- sum(cfu.below)
    sum_dil <- sum(vdilution.below)
    sum_cfulogdil <- sum(log(vdilution.below) * cfu.below)
    sum_logfact <- sum(lgamma(cfu.below + 1))
    sum_cfulogdil_logfact <- sum_cfulogdil - sum_logfact
    logload_firstguess <- log(sum_cfu/sum_dil)
    if (is.infinite(logload_firstguess))
        logload_firstguess <- 0
    dlogLik.ignoreAbove <- function(llambd) {
        lambd <- exp(llambd)
        dll <- -sum_dil + sum_cfu/lambd
        if (!is.na(maxCFU)) {
            for (dd in vdilution.below) {
                avg <- dd * lambd
                la <- dpois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, log = TRUE) -
                  ppois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, lower.tail = TRUE,
                    log.p = TRUE)
                dll <- dll + dd * exp(la)
            }
        }
        return(dll)
    }
    dlogLik.notIgnoreAbove <- function(llambd) {
        lambd <- exp(llambd)
        dll <- -sum_dil + sum_cfu/lambd
        if (!is.na(maxCFU)) {
            for (dd in vdilution.above) {
                avg <- dd * lambd
                la <- dpois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, log = TRUE) -
                  ppois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, lower.tail = FALSE,
                    log.p = TRUE)
                dll <- dll + dd * exp(la)
            }
        }
        return(dll)
    }
    bounds <- logload_firstguess + c(-5, 5)
    if (ignoreAboveMaxCFU) {
        opt <- try(uniroot(dlogLik.ignoreAbove, interval = bounds,
            extendInt = "downX"), silent = TRUE)
    }
    else {
        opt <- try(uniroot(dlogLik.notIgnoreAbove, interval = bounds,
            extendInt = "downX"), silent = TRUE)
    }
    if ("try-error" %in% class(opt) || is.na(opt$estim.prec)) {
        return(list(logload = NA, se.logload = NA, load = NA,
            se.load = NA, code = -1, sumCFU = sum_cfu, maxCFU = maxCFU,
            ignoreAboveMaxCFU = ignoreAboveMaxCFU))
    }
    est <- opt$root
    lambd <- exp(est)
    lambd2 <- lambd * lambd
    d2ll <- -sum_cfu/(lambd2)
    if (!is.na(maxCFU)) {
        if (ignoreAboveMaxCFU) {
            for (dd in vdilution.below) {
                avg <- lambd * dd
                lF <- ppois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, lower.tail = TRUE,
                  log.p = TRUE)
                lf <- dpois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, log = TRUE)
                f_F <- exp(lf - lF)
                d2ll <- d2ll + dd * dd * f_F * (f_F - 1 + maxCFU/avg)
            }
        }
        else {
            for (dd in vdilution.above) {
                avg <- lambd * dd
                lF <- ppois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, lower.tail = FALSE,
                  log.p = TRUE)
                lf <- dpois(maxCFU, lambda = avg, log = TRUE)
                f_F <- exp(lf - lF)
                d2ll <- d2ll - dd * dd * f_F * (f_F + (1 - maxCFU/avg))
            }
        }
    }
    d2ll <- d2ll * c(lambd2, 1)
    se <- ifelse(d2ll < 0, 1/sqrt(-d2ll), NA)
    if (!is.na(logbase) && logbase > 0) {
        sc <- log(logbase)
        est <- est/sc
        se[1] <- se[1]/sc
    }
    return(list(logload = est, se.logload = se[1], load = lambd,
        se.load = se[2], code = 1, sumCFU = sum_cfu, maxCFU = maxCFU,
        ignoreAboveMaxCFU = ignoreAboveMaxCFU))
}


loadEstimate <- function(cfu, dilution, volume = 5, sampleID = NULL,
                         maxCFU = 50, ignoreAboveMaxCFU = TRUE, logbase = NA) {
  if (is.null(sampleID)) {
    res <- loadEstimateOneSample(cfu, dilution, volume, maxCFU,
                                 ignoreAboveMaxCFU, logbase)
    return(res)
  }
  ld <- split(data.frame(CFU = cfu, Dilution = dilution, Volume = volume), sampleID)
  est <- try(sapply(ld, function(dtmp)
    with(dtmp, loadEstimateOneSample(CFU, Dilution, Volume, maxCFU,
                                     ignoreAboveMaxCFU, logbase))))
  res <- data.frame(sampleID = names(ld),
                    logload = as.numeric(est[1, ]),
                    se.logload = as.numeric(est[2, ]),
                    load = as.numeric(est[3, ]),
                    se.load = as.numeric(est[4, ]),
                    code = as.numeric(est[5, ]),
                    sumCFU = as.numeric(est[6, ]),
                    maxCFU = as.numeric(est[7, ]),
                    ignoreAboveMaxCFU = as.numeric(est[8, ]))
  l <- res$code == -2
  if (any(l)) {
    warning(paste("Could not estimate load for", sum(l),
                  "sample(s). Check samples with code=-2!"))
  }
  return(res)
}




Reproducing the per-fly table in R


With the functions above loaded, this reads a counts file saved by the app and a layout file, and gives the same loads, standard errors and detection limits as the Load step, to about 10⁻¹².


reproduce_example.R
## Reproduce the app's per-fly loads in R from a COUNTS file and a layout file.
## Run the two functions above first (loadEstimateOneSample and loadEstimate).

counts <- read.table("COUNTS_plate01.csv", header = TRUE, sep = ";",
                     check.names = FALSE, stringsAsFactors = FALSE)
layout <- read.table("layout.csv", header = TRUE, sep = ";",
                     stringsAsFactors = FALSE)

## A layout by ROW: one fly per row, its dilution series across the columns.
## (For a layout by WELL, merge with by = "WELL" instead.)
counts$ROW <- substr(counts$WELL, 1, 1)
d <- merge(layout, counts, by = "ROW")

d$CFU      <- as.numeric(d$Prett)                          # "Inf" = too many to count
d$dilution <- ifelse(d$Dilution <= 1, 1, 1 / d$Dilution)   # factor 25 -> 1/25
d <- d[!is.na(d$CFU) & !is.na(d$dilution), ]                # ignored or unspotted wells

ceiling_cfu <- 30     # highest count still read reliably
homogenate  <- 250    # uL per fly

est <- loadEstimate(cfu = d$CFU, dilution = d$dilution, volume = d$Volume,
                    sampleID = d$ID, maxCFU = ceiling_cfu,
                    ignoreAboveMaxCFU = FALSE,   # FALSE = censored (the app's default)
                    logbase = 10)

est$CFU_per_fly       <- est$load * homogenate
est$log10_CFU_per_fly <- log10(est$CFU_per_fly)
est$LOD_per_fly       <- homogenate / tapply(d$dilution * d$Volume, d$ID, sum)[est$sampleID]
est


  


  
  

    
CFU Drop Counter


Version 1.0. The version is shown next to the app's name, and written into the progress files and the Load settings record.


CFU Drop Counter counts bacterial colonies (CFU) in 5 µL drops plated on a 96-well plate lid filled with agar, and estimates the bacterial load of each fly from those counts. It runs in a web browser on an iPad, a phone or a computer, with nothing to install. Scans and counts are never uploaded. They stay on the device that opens the app.


Throughout this guide, a drop is the drop of homogenate in one well, and a colony is one CFU in that drop.


The work goes through six steps, shown at the top of the app:


    

  1. Setup: load the scan and describe the plate.
    2. 

  2. Grid: place the drop grid on the scan.
    3. 

  3. Tune: adjust the automatic detection on a sample of drops, then run it on the plate.
    4. 

  4. Count: check and correct each drop by tapping.
    5. 

  5. Export: save the counts as a CSV file.
    6. 

  6. Load: estimate the load per fly from one or several plates and a layout file.
    7. 



The Method tab explains the statistics behind the Load step and gives its R code. Help shows this guide.


Getting started


    

  1. Open the app's address in Safari on the iPad.
    2. 

  2. Tap Share → Add to Home Screen, and from then on open the app from that icon. Safari can clear a website's stored data after 7 days without a visit, but apps opened from the Home Screen are exempt.
    3. 

  3. Put the scans where the iPad can reach them: Files, iCloud Drive or AirDrop. Use the original image files, because Photos and messaging apps can resize images.
    4. 



Scans. Scan by transmitted light (the scanner's transparency unit, light passing through the lid), which gives dark colonies on light agar. Colonies are sharper and better separated than by reflected light, and detection is more accurate. Scans by reflected light (pale colonies on dark agar) still work. About 600 dpi is enough. Save scans as PNG or high-quality JPEG in 8 bits; a 16-bit TIFF of a whole lid can be over 60 MB, opens in Safari only, and adds nothing for counting.


Use example scan on the Setup screen loads an example plate to try the app: a lid scanned by transmitted light, reduced, with the grid already placed.


1. Setup


Plates on this device lists every plate you have started. Each entry shows how many drops you have checked, when the plate was last edited, and whether a CSV or a progress file has been saved for it.


    

  • Open returns to a plate exactly where you left it.
    • 

  • × deletes a plate after you confirm.
    • 

  • New plate starts an empty one.
    • 



Scan. Choose the plate image: PNG or JPEG, or TIFF in Safari only. Choosing a scan while the current plate already has work on it starts a new plate, and the old one stays in the list.


Plate information. These fields become the first columns of the counts file, with one value for the whole plate. Fields can be text, whole numbers, decimals or yes/no, and you can add or remove them. Two field names have a special role:


    

  • Plate name identifies the plate. The Load step uses it to match plates with the layout file. Give every plate its own name with a single number in it, such as plate01. Avoid names like 25-06-17_plate01, because plates can be matched on the number in the name.
    • 

  • Replicate appears next to the plate name in the lists. When it is a whole number, it goes up by one when you count another plate on the same scan.
    • 



Colony types. One row per kind of colony, each with a colour and an optional keyboard key. The default is a single type, Prett. Removing a type also removes the colonies marked with it on the current plate.


Plate format. 8 rows × 12 columns for a 96-well lid. It is locked as soon as the plate has counts, because those counts only make sense in that format. To change it, use Clear all counts of this plate. This can be undone on the Count screen.


Volume and dilution (folded away, because counting does not use them). The volume per drop and the dilution of each well are written into the counts file, and the Load step uses them.


    

  • The dilution can be one value for the whole plate, one per row, or one per column.
    • 

  • Write dilutions as factors: 1 = undiluted, 10 = 1/10, 25 = 1/25.
    • 

  • Leave a box empty for a row or column that was not plated. Those wells get NA as their dilution and are left out of the load estimate.
    • 



The plate information, colony types, format, volume and dilution carry over to the next plate.


2. Grid


    

  1. Drag the four corner markers onto the centres of the corner drops: A1, A12, H1 and H12. A magnifier shows the area under your finger. On a scan holding two plates, Zoom to grid enlarges the plate you are placing.
    2. 

  2. Tap Snap to wells. It fits the grid to the well rims when each circle is already within about a fifth of a well of its place. Undo puts the corners back, and Reset corners starts again.
    3. 



A plate scanned sideways works too: put each marker on the well with that name. For a lid photographed with the dilution series running down the image, A1 is top left, A12 bottom left, H1 top right and H12 bottom right.


Next: tune the detection opens the Tune step.


3. Tune


Tune shows eight drops picked across the plate, from empty to crowded, with the colonies the detection finds in each. Every change of a setting updates them at once, so you can judge a setting on several kinds of drop together. Show other drops picks another eight. Tap a drop to open it in Count.


    

  • 

    Colonies on this scan: pale on dark agar or dark on light agar. The app chooses it from the scan when it is loaded; change it if it guessed wrong. On scans with dark colonies, each drop is compared with its own background; blurred smudges, large dark masses (condensation, a mould) and thin arcs (bubble edges, the well rim) are left out.
    

    • 

  • 

    Image used by the detection: the brightness, contrast and sharpness of the image the detection works on. They help most on dim or low-contrast images; on a sharp, well-lit scan they make little difference.
    

    • 

  • Counting area: the circle in which colonies are detected. Keep it just inside the well rim.
    • 

  • Sensitivity: higher finds fainter colonies; lower ignores faint marks.
    • 

  • Colony size: the expected size of one colony, which decides how merged colonies are split.
    • 



These settings are saved with the plate, and the next plate starts from the same ones.


Detect on all drops runs the detection on the whole plate and opens Count. Count by hand, no detection skips it.


Coming back to Tune later is safe. Once the plate has counts, the main button becomes Detect again on the drops not checked yet. Drops you have looked at or corrected are never touched. Detect again on every drop… replaces those as well, after a confirmation. Either run can be undone with Undo on the Count screen.


4. Count


The drop fills the screen, with the detected colonies circled.


    

  • Tap anywhere on the drop to add a colony of the selected type there.
    • 

  • Tap a mark to remove it. With several colony types, switch Tap a mark to to Change type to give it the selected type instead.
    • 

  • Too many next to a type means the drop cannot be counted for that type. It is saved as Inf.
    • 

  • Empty drop removes the marks and saves the count as 0.
    • 

  • Ignore drop leaves the drop out; it is saved as NA.
    • 

  • Undo and Redo step back and forward through your changes. That covers added or removed colonies, changed types, the drop flags, Clear marks, Clear all counts and re-detections. Each plate keeps its own history of the last 100 steps, even after the app is closed. A new change after an undo starts a new branch, so the steps you undid can no longer be redone.
    • 

  • Move between drops with Previous / Next, by swiping left or right on the drop, or with the plate map, where each well shows its count. On the map, shaded wells were detected but not yet looked at, and wells outlined in green were corrected by hand. Jump to next unchecked drop finds the next drop you have not looked at.
    • 

  • Hold to hide marks (bottom left of the drop) shows the drop without any circles for as long as you hold it, so you can check that no colony is hidden under a mark.
    • 

  • Detect again on this drop reruns the detection on the drop shown, and on no other. Its Sensitivity and Colony size sliders start from the plate's Tune settings on every drop, so they only rescue a difficult drop. To change the settings for the whole plate, go back to Tune.
    • 

  • View: Brightness, Contrast and Sharpness change only what you see on this screen, to check a difficult drop. They do not affect the detection, and they are kept on this device for every plate.
    • 



With a keyboard:


    

  • ← → or space move between drops, and ↑ ↓ move by a row.
    • 

  • The type keys select a colony type, and holding H hides the marks.
    • 

  • Cmd/Ctrl + Z undoes, and Cmd/Ctrl + Shift + Z (or Ctrl + Y) redoes.
    • 



How well detection works. On scans with pale colonies on dark agar, tested on two plates (157 countable drops, compared with manual counts), about 85% of drops came within ±2 colonies, with a mean error of about one colony. On scans with dark colonies on light agar, tested on one plate against hand counts, 77 of the 80 drops with 30 colonies or fewer came within ±2 colonies, with a mean error of about half a colony. The drops that needed correcting had bubbles at the rim, a mould, or colonies spilling past the well rim, which fall outside the counting area. Detection misses colonies in crowded drops where they merge, and occasionally splits a large colony in two. It is a starting point, not a count: look at every drop before exporting. Because every drop is checked, the quality of the detection changes how long counting takes, not the final counts.


5. Export


The Export screen shows a summary of the plate and a table of counts per drop. Shaded cells were detected but not looked at.


Save CSV writes the counts file, named after the scan: COUNTS_<scan name>.csv.


    

  • When several plates come from the same scan, the plate name is added: COUNTS_<scan>_<plate>.csv.
    • 

  • If those plates also share a name, the replicate is added as well.
    • 

  • You can edit the name before saving.
    • 

  • On an iPad the file goes to Files → Downloads.
    • 



Save progress file writes a backup of the plate (see Saving and backups).


Count another plate on this scan starts the next plate on the same image, keeping the grid so you only need to move it. New plate with another scan starts a fresh plate. Both keep the current plate in the list.

The Load step estimates the bacterial load of each fly from all of that fly's drops, by maximum likelihood. Open it after counting. The layout is only read here, so you stay blind to the treatments while you count.

Settings


    

  • Colony type to estimate, or all types added together.
    • 

  • Drops with more colonies than can be read. Counts stop being reliable once colonies merge, so drops above a ceiling (default 30) need special handling. There are three options, and they are different estimators rather than stricter or looser versions of one:
    • 

  • Keep them as counts known to exceed the ceiling (default). Each such drop contributes only the fact that it held more than 30 colonies. This is a censored Poisson model, and it uses every drop.
    • 

  • Leave them out, and correct the rest for the ceiling. The remaining counts are modelled as Poisson counts known not to exceed the ceiling. This is a right-truncated Poisson model.
    • 

  • No ceiling. Every finite count is used as it stands.
    • 

  • Homogenate: µL per fly (default 250), used to scale the load to a whole fly.
    • 

  • Log base: log10 or log2. It rescales the results and changes no fit.
    • 

  • Dilution twice: shown only when a fly has the same dilution more than once. Choose whether to use both copies or only the first.
    • 



Why drops above the ceiling are kept by default. In simulations of the lab's 10-dilution design, keeping them gave errors as small or smaller than leaving them out, at every load. Near the top of the measurable range, where a fly often has a single countable drop, leaving them out sometimes gave estimates more than 10 times too high; keeping them did not. The Method tab gives the simulation, the numbers and the reason.

    •

How the estimate is made


Each drop's colony count is modelled as Poisson, with mean equal to the fly's load per µL multiplied by the drop's volume and dilution. The load is estimated by maximum likelihood over all of the fly's drops. With the default setting this is a censored Poisson model, and with Leave them out a right-truncated Poisson model.


The Method tab gives the full description: the likelihood for each setting, the standard error, the detection limit and the assumptions. It also shows the R code of the estimator, the lab's loadEstimate(), which the app runs translated into JavaScript, and an R example that reproduces the per-fly table from a counts file and a layout file. The translation agrees with the R code to about 10⁻¹².


Saving and backups


Everything is saved automatically, at every tap, in the browser's storage on the device. This storage is not a file you can see in Files. The app reads it by itself, so when you reopen the app you are back where you stopped.


A web page cannot write files by itself on an iPad. Every file it saves needs you to confirm it, goes to Downloads, and cannot be updated or deleted by the app afterwards.


The progress file (PROGRESS_<scan name>.json) is a backup you save by hand. It holds the marks, the flags, the grid position, the plate settings and the Tune settings. It does not hold the image or the undo history. Save one from time to time, and keep it next to the scan.


To continue a plate on another device, or after the iPad's data was lost:


    

  1. Copy the progress file and the original scan to that device.
    2. 

  2. Open the app and go to Setup.
    3. 

  3. Tap Open progress file… and choose the scan, in either order.
    4. 



The app checks the scan's pixel size, so the marks cannot land on the wrong image. The reopened plate is added to the list as a plate of its own. The two devices do not stay in sync, so keep working on one of them. The layout file and the Load settings are not in the progress file.
